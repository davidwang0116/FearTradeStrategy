### Does VIX add information beyond the QQQ selloff?

I took the 28 primary episode starts as given. For each, I looked only in the same study period for signal dates with VIX below 24, QQQ's drawdown from its trailing 252-session closing high within **2 percentage points**, and its trailing 21-session annualized realized volatility within **5 points**. All features use data through the candidate close. I ranked candidates by the sum of squared differences scaled by those tolerances. I took the three closest, skipping any whose 63-session returns overlapped the focal trade or another chosen control for that trade. Both the episode and the controls use next-close purchases, 63 sessions in QQQ, and 5 bp per side. I required three matches and gave each matched episode equal weight.

| Period | Episodes matched | Mean episode trade | Mean of comparable lower-VIX trades | Difference | Episodes beating controls |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2008–2019 | 15 of 16 | 6.41% | 6.00% | +0.41 pp | 7 of 15 |
| 2020–2026 | 11 of 12 | 5.19% | 6.47% | −1.28 pp | 5 of 11 |

June 11, 2008 and August 5, 2024 each had only two qualifying controls. Both were excluded. Control dates can recur or overlap across different episodes. This is a retrospective diagnostic with analyst-chosen matching tolerances. It uses both earlier and later dates within each period and estimates no causal effect. On the covered events, a lower-VIX day with similar QQQ conditions was already a competitive entry.

Two additional comparisons put that result in context.

- On the same dates with complete QQQ and SPY outcomes, first-trigger excess over each asset's own average trade was +1.67 / +0.21 points for QQQ and +1.44 / +0.03 for SPY in training / later. These use a common coverage end and require the *signal* date to be inside the period. That explains the small difference from the headline QQQ benchmark. The two assets share the same crises.
- I froze the passive comparator's extra QQQ weight at the tactical sleeve's **training** average. The 7.5% trigger policy earned 0.703 Sharpe later versus 0.714 for a passive extra 2.52% QQQ allocation. The 30% policy earned 0.724 versus 0.717 for an extra 7.11%. Those later accounts start fresh. The passive comparator uses the repository's daily constant-weight calculation, which omits rebalancing costs. The differences are too small and inconsistent to establish a timing advantage.

## Original review before the corrections

**Keep immediate purchase at VIX ≥24 followed by a 63-session hold as the research benchmark. The results do not justify allocating a permanent cash reserve specifically to that trade.** High-VIX days in this sample often preceded recoveries. An investor gets a substantially smaller advantage when buying once per episode, and the funded account earns approximately the same Sharpe as a passive account. Of the ideas examined here, managing how much equity risk the core carries deserves the next test.

## What the strategy earned

The first two rows below count every qualifying day. The third counts one trade after VIX has calmed and risen again. Spreads are percentage points above the average eligible QQQ trade in the corresponding study.

| Measurement | 2008–2019 | 2020–2026 | What changes the interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Every close with VIX ≥24, buy QQQ for 63 sessions | +3.51 | +3.90 | Later spread is −0.51 outside 2020. |
| Every close with VIX ≥24 and 20-day easing | +6.55 | +5.37 | One 2020 episode supplies 64 of 128 later dates. Equal-weighting the nine episodes gives +0.40. Excluding all 2020 dates gives −3.59. |
| One trade per VIX ≥24 episode, expanding-median reset | +1.68 | +0.32 | Mean net trades earned 5.50% and 5.64%. The later trade beat an average-date trade 6 times in 12. Removing the best later trade changes the spread to −1.95. |
| One trade per VIX ≥24 episode, 21 quiet sessions to reset | −0.86 | −0.23 | The counts are still 16 and 12. Changing *which* dates qualify changes the conclusion. |

The later sample's best primary trade started April 3, 2025 and earned 30.69%. The first COVID trade illustrates the risk more clearly. VIX triggered on February 24, 2020. QQQ filled on February 25 at an adjusted 207.56, fell to 163.16 on March 16, then exited May 26 at 221.20. The trade earned **6.47% net** after enduring a **21.43% loss from the initial budget** at its worst close. An investor who counted many later rebound days as separate successful calls would be describing a different experience. VIX itself measures the option market's expectation of 30-day S&P 500 volatility. Forecasting the direction of QQQ over three months is a separate empirical hypothesis. The 7.5% sleeve occupied 3.39% of the fresh later account on an average day. The 30% version occupied 8.58% and had insufficient cash for the full order on 11 of 12 later triggers. Its higher CAGR came with greater volatility and a deeper drawdown. Against a passive account holding the same average tactical QQQ weight, the later Sharpe differences were **−0.011** for the smaller order and **+0.006** for the larger one. Their paired 90% block-bootstrap intervals were −0.046 to +0.027 and −0.087 to +0.118. That comparator uses the realized mean sleeve weight, so it is an attribution tool constructed in hindsight. ## Where implementation and intent diverge

1. **The fixed-delay control is conditional.** The plan explicitly has E6 always buy its second half five sessions after the first. The code cancels if fear normalizes, and the test requires that cancellation. It skipped 3 of 16 training and 2 of 12 later second buys. I recalculated E6 with both half-orders, the same deadline, costs and cash interest. Its mean difference versus buying everything immediately changes from **−0.30 to +0.07 points** in training and **−0.11 to −0.48** later. It wins 50% in each sample. The 5-day confirmation strategy E5 trails this corrected control by 0.82 and 0.29 points. Immediate entry still survives the selection rule. Five-day easing confirmed before cancellation in 22 of 28 episodes. The 20-day reference confirmed in zero within the 10-session window and eight over the longer 63-session entry test. [Control implementation](research/tactical.py#L76) · [Control test](tests/test_vix_tactical.py#L46)

2. **Some reserve orders survive the end of their fear episode.** E5 and E7 cancel pending orders at the first calm close. E1 and E3 can buy again if fear returns before their old deadline. The March 6, 2025 E1 plan placed a rung on April 7, the same close as the initial buy for the separate April 3 episode. A mandate that keeps each 63-session buy program open can allow that, but it needs a shared cash budget. If the intended mandate ends unfilled orders on normalization, the later timing advantage over immediate full deployment falls from **+0.63 to +0.21** for E1, **+0.10 to −0.24** for the scaled ladder and **+2.24 to +1.38** for E3. All three still lose in training. E1 also anchors the decline to the previous signal close. Specify the actual fill as the anchor if the intended promise is “buy 8% below what we paid.” [Ladder and scheduled purchases](research/tactical.py#L82)

3. **One sensitivity has two episode definitions.** Both drivers test the 504-session VIX rank ≥80% with VIX ≥20. The tactical driver re-arms when that rolling rank falls to 50%. The all-signals driver re-arms floor-based rules when the VIX level falls to its long-history median. That gives **13 versus 28 training episodes** for the same entry condition. The primary VIX ≥24 comparison is consistent. Give each reset a distinct label or share one implementation before comparing sensitivities. The composite sensitivity also has 14 training and **6** later complete 63-session episodes in this run, versus the plan's estimate of 14 and 5. [Tactical triggers](research/run_tactical.py#L51) · [All-signal triggers](research/run_triggers.py#L63)

4. **The standalone comparison gives some indicators access to an impossible benchmark history.** The all-signals driver computes the average-date benchmark across every session in the period. VIX3M eligibility begins in September 2009. Restricting the benchmark's signal dates to actual coverage changes its inverted-curve training spread from **+2.10 to +1.65 points** and the flat-curve spread from **+2.99 to +2.54**. The family shuffle also permits placements before that input existed. The earlier daily A/B study already aligns coverage. Reuse that behavior for the standalone benchmark and its null distribution. [Standalone benchmark](research/run_triggers.py#L99) · [Family test](research/run_triggers.py#L201)

5. **The older v2 engine can sell QLD on the same close that reveals its take-profit.** It marks QLD with today's return before testing the 60% gain and executing. Using yesterday's marked gain while keeping the other rules fixed moved the Sharpe values by less than 0.001 for the reference, 10-day, composite and no-tranche rows I checked. It matters for execution integrity and does little to explain the weak results. A larger attribution issue is that tranche and reinvestment buys go only into the unguarded core. Also, when the guard is enabled, 35% of starting NAV goes into guarded QQQ and the unguarded 35% is split between QQQ and SPY. That starts v2 at **52.5% QQQ / 17.5% SPY**, versus 35% / 35% in the clean sleeve. [Core setup](../research/strategy.py#L92) · [QLD return and sale](../research/strategy.py#L110) · [Core purchases](../research/strategy.py#L128)

6. **Cash and Sharpe need a common accounting convention.** The panel uses one approximate annual-average T-bill rate throughout each calendar year. The reported Sharpe uses excess mean divided by total-return volatility, while the bootstrap divides by excess-return volatility. The differences are small here, but both numbers should estimate the same quantity. Use previously available daily bill rates, convert the discount quote into an accrual, and share one Sharpe function. ## What the rejected rules teach

- **Of the VIX features, short easing is the most defensible one to keep observing.** With VIX ≥24, five- and ten-session easing retain just +0.44 and +0.53 points of day-weighted later spread outside 2020. Their standalone first-entry spreads are negative in training and large later on only five and four episodes. The term-structure ranking reverses between periods. An intraday spike to 24 that fades by the close underperforms an average date in both periods. The breadth proxy has only three confirmed training days, credit starts in late 2023, and actual point-in-time constituent breadth is absent. None supplies a sufficiently evidenced extra gate. E2's percentage-of-remaining-cash sizing and the stricter composite are deferred ideas. VIX ≥30 appears in the signal studies, while the full entry/exit plan uses ≥24 as its primary trigger.
- **Waiting changes both price and participation.** E5 with 5-day easing loses 0.75 and 0.77 points versus immediate purchase. Its average worst training loss from initial capital improves from 6.13% to 4.73%. Requiring a QQQ five-close breakout for the second half costs about 2.1 points in both periods. This can be a deliberate reduction in interim losses. The investor pays for it by waiting or leaving cash idle.
- **Timing value can be positive while the investor earns less money.** E3, the ten-session staging rule, looks strongest among the later reserve plans at +2.24 points on the study's timing measure. It deployed 48% of its budget and earned **5.12%** versus **5.64%** for spending the full reserve at once. In training those returns were **2.60% versus 5.50%**. The timing measure deducts average QQQ returns for each tranche's actual holding length. It is a useful secondary diagnostic. The investor's primary result is the net return on the funded reserve.
- **The return screen can reject something a risk-conscious investor values.** The original 10-session breakdown exit lost 6.40 points in training and 4.49 later. Arming it only after a recovery reduced the training shortfall to 3.49 points and gave +0.16 later. At 7.5% funded size the armed exit scored Sharpe **0.670 versus 0.673** for holding in training, and **0.734 versus 0.703** later. Its later advantage over its own exposure-matched control was +0.020, with a 90% interval of −0.003 to +0.047. Under the written return objective it loses. Under a Sharpe objective it remains an unconfirmed candidate.
- **A 126-session exit table doesn't directly answer how long to hold.** The code compares early sales with holding to *each* cap. I directly compared 126 sessions with selling at 63 and earning bills to the same day 126. On common episodes, holding longer adds **0.61 points in training** and **5.91 later**. Removing the best training episode changes the training difference to −0.32 points. Part of the later gain is simply another quarter of equity exposure. The current evidence establishes no optimal holding period. [Exit comparisons](research/run_tactical.py#L220)
- **There were fewer independent tests than the table suggests.** Only 26 of 42 triggers met the eight-episode training minimum. Eleven passed. Jointly shifting their dates produced 7.36 passes on average, at least 11 in 28% of shifts, and a best result as strong as observed in 75%. None of those 11 passed all three conditions later. Of 252 signal-and-exit pairs, 96 were automatically ineligible under the same minimum. Even primary 63-session trades overlap across three pairs of consecutive training starts and five later pairs. The episode bootstrap treats the resampled episodes as independent. For the earlier 1,116 distinct grid rules, the 0.09 training/later rank correlation and the 2020 concentration argue against choosing a new cutoff from that grid. [Family results](results/trigger_family.csv) · [Episode ledger](results/trigger_episodes.csv)
- **The early stress test earns its place in the conclusion.** Using the full VIX history to establish the initial episode state gives five triggers during QQQ history before 2008. Four trades also finished before 2008. Their net returns were **−23.22%, −23.46%, −19.34% and +11.03%**, averaging **−13.75%**. The November 2007 trigger exited in February 2008. This reconciles the plan's five triggers and the attachment's four trades. These episodes have already been examined. The removed alternative v2 guards and exits have no retained implementation or result tables in the reviewed tree. The attachment's 0.921 for an alternative guard is a post-selection result whose exact implementation I could not verify.

## Where I would look for higher Sharpe

**First test the core exposure decision.** I isolated the existing guard's one rule in two accounts with identical starting holdings. Each began with 52.5% QQQ, 17.5% SPY and 30% bills. In one, a 35% initial QQQ position switched to bills at the next close when QQQ was below 97% of its 200-session average, and returned on the reverse signal. Each switch cost 5 bp. Holdings drifted continuously from 2008, with no tranches, QLD or greed trims. I used the project's existing threshold without a parameter search.

| Identical starting assets | Training Sharpe | Training CAGR | Training max drawdown | Later carried Sharpe | Later CAGR | Later max drawdown |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Passive | 0.713 | 10.25% | −33.8% | 0.742 | 18.21% | −31.5% |
| Trend guard on the 35% position | 0.754 | 9.26% | −21.7% | 0.869 | 18.22% | −23.1% |

That is a larger historical effect than the VIX sleeve produced. It also shows the price of the protection in training. The later paired Sharpe improvement of +0.127 has a 90% interval of approximately −0.019 to +0.281 using 63-session blocks. This is an exploratory attribution exercise on known history. A passive comparator scaled to the same risk using training data would be the next hurdle.

1. **Freeze a small portfolio comparison.** Run passive, guard only, VIX sleeve only, and guard plus VIX. Give them identical starting QQQ/SPY holdings, one stated reserve and rebalancing policy, identical fees and daily bills. Assign new equity purchases to guarded and unguarded holdings by an explicit rule. Keep QLD out of this comparison, then measure its incremental effect separately.
2. **Ask what VIX adds beyond buying a selloff.** Within each training fold, compare episode entries with dates at similar trailing QQQ drawdown and realized volatility. Exclude overlapping forward-return windows from controls and report how many episodes have a credible match. Include both a passive equity/bill mix and a mechanical rebalancing strategy with a comparable risk budget. A contrarian buy rule has to justify itself against what ordinary rebalancing already buys.
3. **Use one portfolio objective and one episode diagnostic.** For a higher-Sharpe mandate, decide on net *incremental* Sharpe against the risk-matched control, with a predeclared drawdown limit and minimum worthwhile improvement. Show excess return, volatility, maximum drawdown, turnover, actual cash spent and paired intervals. Keep paired episode P&L to explain the result. For sparse reserve rules, report both the incremental return across all episodes and the comparison on episodes where orders actually differed. Counting ties as losses makes a 60% all-episode win threshold mathematically impossible for an add-on that fires in fewer than 60% of episodes.
4. **Protect the next test from repeated selection.** Use expanding chronological folds with a gap long enough that every training trade has finished, 126 sessions plus the execution lag for a 126-session hold. Keep overlapping episodes together when resampling, report results with each major crisis removed, and log every specification tried. Freeze one candidate for data arriving after the research cutoff. Both reported periods and the pre-2008 stress test have already influenced the discussion.
